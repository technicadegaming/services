Arcade Ticket Printer Stops Intermittently: What to Check Before Replacing It

Troubleshoot repeat printer stoppages using power, cable, sensor, paper path and controller checks.

Technicade independent technical resources · Use model-specific service documentation

Share on Facebook

Quick diagnostic path

An intermittent printer problem is easier to isolate when you record exactly how it stops. Does the printer lose power, jam the paper, stop receiving commands, or print but fail to count tickets? Keep those symptoms separate.

Capture the failure conditions

Record model, paper type, firmware where available, operating temperature, time since startup, error indicators, ticket count, and whether the issue occurs only after several payouts. A video of the actual failure can be more useful than a video of a normal test print.

Inspect the paper path safely

Shut down according to the manufacturer. Check for misrouted paper, worn rollers, paper dust, damaged cutter parts and incorrectly positioned ticket or paper sensors. Never place fingers near powered cutters or moving gears.

Separate power from communication

A power block change does not prove the printer is receiving stable power at the printer connector under load. Equally, a working display does not prove the game is sending ticket commands. Qualified technicians should use approved service measurements and diagnostic prints, taking care not to short connectors.

Test cables and connectors

Inspect bent contacts, latch fit, strain relief and cable routing. If the manual permits it, use a known-good compatible cable and compare results after enough print cycles to reproduce the original fault. Don't substitute a cable based on appearance alone; the pinout matters.

Check sensors and counting

When paper moves but the game reports missing tickets, inspect optical sensors, ticket notch alignment if applicable, and counter signals. Compare printer test output with the game payout command and redemption accounting. Keep physical output and recorded award counts synchronized.

Plan the next step

If the fault follows the printer when moved to a verified compatible test station, focus on printer-side causes. If the fault stays with the cabinet, investigate the harness, supply, or controller. Document every swap and result so you don't replace a functioning printer unnecessarily.
