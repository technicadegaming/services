Is a Motion-Based Arcade Shooter Worth Buying? How FEC Operators Can Judge Revenue

A practical way to assess guest appeal, utilization, earning potential, floor-space costs and maintenance before purchasing a motion shooter arcade game.

Quick answer

A smooth gun and motion system can make a strong first impression, but it does not prove that the game will earn well in a specific location. Ask operators for actual weekly gross, price per play, repeat-play rates, uptime and occupied floor space, then compare those numbers with your existing attractions. Fun to play is a starting point, not a revenue forecast

An arcade shooter with responsive aiming, a moving platform and strong immersion can be memorable. But a game that impressed a guest at Dave & Buster's may perform differently in a smaller family entertainment center with different foot traffic, pricing, group sizes and competing attractions.

The original community post didn't include a readable model name, so this is intentionally a game-selection guide rather than a review of a named machine.

Ask for real operator numbers

Before buying, ask other operators for:

  • Average weekly gross and the range between slow and busy weeks.
  • Price per play and average play-session duration.
  • Approximate plays per day, repeat plays and peak wait times.
  • Number of simultaneous players and whether spectators convert into players.
  • Uptime, routine maintenance, gun calibration and motion-system service history.
  • Consumable parts, warranty terms, supplier support and typical repair response.
  • Footprint including required safety clearance and queueing space.

Numbers from a similar market or venue type are much more useful than unverified hype.

Compare the correct measures

A high weekly gross is less impressive if the game occupies substantially more floor area, costs more to operate, or is unavailable frequently. Compare gross per occupied square foot, contribution after card and operating costs, and how long it may take to recover the purchase and installation expense.

Estimate payback using your own expected operating weeks, vendor quote and realistic uptime. For an unfamiliar title, use conservative, middle and optimistic scenarios rather than a single promised figure.

Consider operational reliability

Motion platforms add experience but may also add maintenance and safety checks. Ask about parts availability, motion actuators, emergency-stop behavior, service access, gun sensor reliability, software updates and the manufacturer’s maintenance requirements.

Check whether a venue has sufficient space and supervision for the age groups it attracts. Obtain the official installation instructions before committing to the floor plan.

Collect better feedback from the community

A productive operator discussion includes the manufacturer and exact model, how long each location has operated the game, venue size, actual gross rather than just rankings, and whether the owners would buy it again.

An anonymous revenue ranking like "top five" can be informative, but it is not a substitute for actual costs, gross and uptime.

Decision checklist

  • Obtain the manufacturer/model and current distributor quotation.
  • Get real measurements from two or more comparable operators.
  • Check local demand and nearby competing attractions.
  • Estimate operating cost and service response time.
  • Compare results to your existing best alternative for that floor space.
  • Decide whether to buy, lease, run a limited test or wait.
